VELO turns warehouse receiving from a paper checklist into a verified digital control point: every delivery is checked against what was ordered, every discrepancy is evidenced, and inventory updates only after a human confirms what physically arrived.

Why

A tick on paper proves a form was filled. It does not prove the goods arrived, matched, or were usable.

How

Three-way match: Ordered vs Supplier-claimed vs Physically received — compared live at the dock, on a phone.

Proof

Inventory is created by a confirmed count — never by copying a delivery note. Every exception has photo, reason, and approver.

01

Why — the tick is not confirmation

When goods arrive at a Vietnamese warehouse today, the process is manual, rigid, and exhausting — and it creates inventory the business cannot trust.

What happens today

Truck arrives with paper delivery note (phiếu giao hàng)

Employee checks under time pressure, driver waiting

Visual check — often without the purchase order beside them

Cartons ticked on paper; damage, shortages, near-expiry noted by hand — or not at all

Paper filed; result re-typed into Excel hours or days later

System now says goods are in stock — whether they are or not

A tick does not prove…

  • Counted quantity = claimed quantity
  • Right SKU, unit, pack size, specification
  • Goods undamaged and acceptable
  • Which batch arrived, or when it expires
  • Who counted it, when, under what exception
  • Anything, when the supplier disputes it later

Root cause (not careless people)

  • Order, delivery note, physical goods — three sources, never systematically compared
  • Verification depends on memory and handwriting under pressure
  • Documentation happens after the event, by re-typing
  • Exceptions — the costly cases — have no structured record

Who feels it

  • Receiver: counts, inspects, unloads, does paperwork simultaneously — then gets blamed when stock vanishes
  • Manager: hears about damage hours later via chat; cannot win supplier claims
  • Sales: sells stock that was assumed, not real → cancelled orders
  • Owner/finance: pays invoices for goods never verified
→

One bad delivery poisons everything

Receiving is treated as paperwork.

03

How — three-way match

The mechanism judges should remember: three sources compared for every delivery line. Stock exists only when they agree — or a named human approves the difference.

1 · Ordered

Purchase order / expected delivery. What the business asked for.

+
2 · Claimed

Supplier delivery note, read by AI OCR. What the supplier says arrived.

+
3 · Received

Physically scanned & counted by the employee. What is actually here.

=
Verified stock

Digital phiếu nhập kho + inventory + audit trail. Nothing by assumption.

MATCH
Ordered = claimed = received, condition good → receiver confirms.
ATTENTION
Short/over quantity, near-expiry batch, no-PO line → structured reason required.
MISMATCH
Wrong item, damage, large variance → photo + manager approval before stock moves.

The 8-step dock workflow

Expected delivery created from PO/CSV — or no-PO mode when none exists

Scan delivery note — AI OCR extracts supplier, lines, quantities; human confirms

Scan & count goods — barcode/QR; fallback: search + photo + printable QR label

Live 3-way match — system does arithmetic; human judges exceptions

Confirm or flag — identity + timestamp; damage needs photo; driver co-confirms

Manager approves — accept / partial / reject / open supplier claim

Digital receipt — phiếu nhập kho auto-generated; documentation is the by-product

Monitor — live stock, batch/expiry (FEFO), low-stock and supplier-accuracy alerts

AI does (perception)

  • OCR on Vietnamese/English delivery notes & labels
  • Fuzzy match supplier names → internal SKUs (“Keo Sika 11FC 600ml” → SKF-11FC-600)
  • Anomaly hints; later: forecasting

Rules do (control)

  • Match arithmetic, variance thresholds
  • Expiry windows, approval routing
  • Receipt generation

Deliberate: control logic must be predictable and auditable.

Humans do (judgement)

  • Count, inspect, photograph
  • Confirm matches
  • Approve every exception

VELO recommends and records. People decide. No silent stock changes.

Impact — mechanisms first, numbers honestly

We claim mechanisms now. We claim numbers only after a pilot measures them against a real baseline.

Errors stop at the door

Short, wrong, or damaged goods caught during receiving — not discovered weeks later.

Documentation is free

Receipt, audit trail, and stock update are by-products of checking — not evening re-typing.

Evidence wins disputes

Photo + reason + driver co-confirmation + approver, timestamped. Claims resolve on facts.

Stock can be trusted

Sales promises, reorders, and payments run on confirmed quantities. Receivers get protection, not blame.

07

Weaknesses & proof — our defence plan

Every weakness below has a mitigation in the product and an evidence task. Tick tasks off as the team completes them (saved in this browser).

Evidence we must collect (E1–E6)

0 / 6 evidence tasks done

Known weaknesses → mitigation → admit

  • No scannable barcode: search + photo + printable internal QR labels. Admit: first receipt of unlabelled goods is slower.
  • Messy supplier notes/handwriting: OCR always human-confirmed; fuzzy SKU matching learns confirmed pairs. Admit: poor handwriting limits OCR.
  • Bulk goods (cement, timber): count by pallet/unit + stack photo + variance tolerance. Admit: VELO verifies declared counts; it cannot weigh a truck.
  • “Surveillance” resistance: frame audit as receiver protection, supplier facts, not personal blame. Admit: adoption needs manager behaviour change + training.
  • Messy SKU catalogue: CSV clean-up, pilot starts with top SKUs by volume. Admit: data clean-up is real upfront work.
  • Dock connectivity: offline-tolerant design requirement. Admit: roadmap until demonstrated — never pitch as built.

Judge objections — one-line defences

  • “Just a digital checklist?” A checklist records ticks; VELO enforces 3-way match, blocks silent stock creation, and structures exceptions with approval.
  • “Where’s the AI?” In perception — reading VN documents, resolving supplier naming chaos. Control arithmetic is rules on purpose: auditable.
  • “WMS/ERP already does this?” At enterprise cost and timelines. Our segment runs paper + Excel; VELO is phone-first, days to deploy, and can feed an ERP later.
  • “Employees can fake counts?” No system prevents collusion — we say so. Identity stamps, photos, driver co-confirmation, and approval raise the cost and expose patterns.
  • “Suppliers won’t cooperate?” They change nothing; VELO also documents their correct deliveries, which protects them.
  • “Impact numbers from where?” Labelled pilot targets with a measurement method and an 8-week gate — not results.

Responsible AI & risk rules

  • Inventory and supplier-pricing data are sensitive: role-based access (receiver/manager); all collected evidence anonymised before it touches a slide.
  • AI output is always human-confirmed before it affects stock; confidence shown, errors correctable, corrections improve matching.
  • No automatic supplier payment or stock write-off — named humans confirm counts and approve exceptions.
  • Failure mode: if scan/OCR fails, manual count still completes. VELO degrades to a structured form; it never blocks a delivery.
