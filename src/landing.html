// 01 — WHAT VELO DOES

One app. Four jobs. Zero guesswork.

Staff scan the barcode that is already on every product. Velo remembers where things are, what is running low and what expires soon, then tells the person what to do next. People always make the final call. Velo only suggests.

01DELIVERIES

Check in deliveries, store them right

Scan the pallet when the truck arrives. Velo suggests the best spot in the back room and records the expiry date. No paper forms at the loading dock.

SCAN → SPOT → DONE
02SHELVES

No more empty shelves

When customers buy the last few items, a staff phone gets one clear job: which box, from where, and how many. The oldest stock goes out first, so less is thrown away.

SALE → TASK → REFILLED
03CUSTOMERS

Answers in seconds

Staff can ask "Is this board approved for a fire wall?" or "Where is the extra stock?" and get an answer in under two seconds, based on the store's own product sheets and building rules.

ASK → ANSWER IN < 2S
04LATER · ALL STORES

Stores help each other

When one store runs out, Velo finds the item in a nearby store and prepares a transfer. The manager approves it with one click.

FIND → APPROVE → MOVE

// 03 — HOW IT'S BUILT

Four building blocks. All in-house.

Everything runs on Company A's own computers, not on someone else's cloud. Prices, stock levels and supplier details never leave the company, and stores keep working even if the internet goes down.

  1. 01
    ON THE PHONE

    Staff app

    Any smartphoneNothing to installNext.js

    WHAT IT DOESTurns any store smartphone into a barcode scanner that also tells staff what to do: check in deliveries, put stock away and refill shelves.

    WHY IT MATTERSReplaces scanner guns that cost $1,800 or more each. Staff open it in the phone's browser, and basic scanning keeps working without Wi-Fi.

  2. 02
    THE BRAIN

    Store hub

    Instant alertsConnected to the tillsFastAPI

    WHAT IT DOESThe central system that knows what is in stock, where it is and who is doing what. When a till sells something, it sends the right task to the right person straight away.

    WHY IT MATTERSTasks arrive the moment they are needed, so nobody has to keep checking a screen or a paper list.

  3. 03
    THE MEMORY

    Store records

    WHY IT MATTERSAll store information sits in one place, so it is simple to back up and easy for the IT team to check.

// 06 — QUESTIONS

Fair questions, straight answers

What is Velo, in one sentence?

A phone app that helps staff in building-supply stores check in deliveries, keep shelves full, sell older stock first and answer customer questions, all by scanning the barcodes already on the products.

Why start with store staff instead of delivery trucks?

Every delivery, transfer and online order depends on knowing exactly what is on the shelf and in the back room. Staff create that information every time they move stock. Get it right inside the store first; otherwise you only automate the mess.

Does the AI make decisions on its own?

No. Velo suggests and reminds, and a person confirms every action with a scan or a tap. It never changes stock numbers, throws anything away or places an order by itself.

Is an AI running inside the company fast enough?

Yes. A small open-source AI on one in-house computer answers questions in under two seconds. The everyday work (scans, refill alerts, task lists) does not depend on the AI at all, so stores keep running even if the AI is switched off.

What happens to the old scanner guns?

They are replaced by ordinary rugged smartphones that scan with the camera. A scanner gun costs $1,500–$2,200 plus about 20% a year in maintenance, so hardware costs drop by more than 75%.

How does one system work in three different countries?

One system, set up for each country. Denmark starts with paper-free deliveries, Norway with one standard way of organising stores, and Sweden adds home delivery last.
