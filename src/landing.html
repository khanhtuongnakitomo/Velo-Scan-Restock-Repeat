A tick is not a confirmation.

A warehouse can only sell what it actually has, yet inventory truth is created at the receiving door, where the process is weakest.

→

Receiving today

  1. A truck arrives with a paper delivery note.
  2. It is checked under time pressure while the driver waits.
  3. Goods are compared by eye, often without the purchase order.
  4. Damage and short counts go into handwriting, chat, or nowhere.
  5. The paper is re-typed into Excel later, if it survives.
  6. Inventory says the goods are in stock, whether or not they are.
→

What a tick does not prove

- ticked = true
+ counted == claimed
+ product == ordered code, unit, pack
+ condition == undamaged
+ batch, expiry recorded
+ who, when, which exception
+ evidence if disputed

Receiving is treated as paperwork, not as a control point.

→

One unverified delivery

  • Sales promises stock that is not there.
  • Purchasing reorders from false quantities.
  • Warehouse finds expired or damaged stock too late.
  • Finance pays for goods never received.

FAQ

Isn't this just a digital checklist or inventory app?
A checklist records ticks. VELO enforces a three-way match between the order, the supplier's claim and the physical count, blocks silent stock creation, and structures exceptions with evidence and approval before inventory changes.
Where is the AI? Matching quantities is just rules.
Correct, and deliberately so: control arithmetic should be deterministic and auditable. AI does the perception work rules cannot: reading Vietnamese delivery documents and resolving supplier naming chaos into product codes.
Warehouse software already does receiving. Why VELO?
Enterprise systems do, with scanners and months of setup. VELO is phone-first, deploys in days, exports to the tools businesses already use, and can later feed an ERP instead of competing with it.
What if employees just fake the counts?
No system fully prevents collusion. VELO raises the cost of error and fraud: identity-stamped confirmations, photo evidence, driver co-confirmation, manager approval and an audit trail that makes patterns visible.
Do suppliers have to change anything?
No. Suppliers keep their delivery notes. The driver only confirms a discrepancy that is already physically in front of both parties.
What happens when the scan or the AI fails?
The receiver can always complete a manual count. VELO degrades to a structured digital form and never blocks a delivery.
